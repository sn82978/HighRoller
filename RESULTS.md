### Reading the trading table

Two things stand out and both belong in the report.

**Buy-and-hold has a genuinely positive gross edge, and loses anyway.** Backing
the favourite at the open earns **+$20.87 per $1,000 deployed before costs** and
wins 57.3% of markets. Fees take **$30.73**, so it lands at **−$9.86**. This is
the cleanest demonstration of the project's thesis anywhere in our results: a
real, positive predictive edge, destroyed by transaction costs alone.

**Buy-and-hold beats XGBoost by a wide margin** — −$9.86 vs −$34.88 per $1,000,
and +$20.87 vs −$3.61 on gross. The model's threshold rule pays essentially the
same fees (31.27 vs 30.73 per $1,000) while producing a *negative* gross edge.
Since the model is a near-copy of the market price, `|p_hat − p_mkt| > 0.01` is
firing on noise rather than signal, and it picks the side that noise points to.
The dumbest policy in the study is the best of the three that trade.

**No-trade still beats both.** Zero is greater than −9.86 and −34.88.

## Metric definitions, from the code

Every definition below was read off the executing code, not the docstrings.

| metric | how it is actually computed | file:line |
|---|---|---|
| `win_rate` | **Per market**, and only over markets that took a position: `(np.array([r.pnl for r in traded]) > 0).mean()`, where `traded = [r for r in results if r.traded]` and each `r` is one market. Not per trade. | `metrics.py:294`, `:263` |
| `avg_holding_candles` | **From actual entry/exit events.** `MarketResult.holding_candles = exit_candle - entry_candle`; `entry_candle` is set on the first filled buy and `exit_candle` on either an early close or settlement. NaN when the policy never entered, and `np.nanmean` skips those. It is *not* a row or step count. | `metrics.py:305`, `:207-211`; `backtest.py:134-146` |
| `turnover` | **From actual fills.** `notional / deployed`, where `notional_traded` sums `shares * price` over trades executed *before* settlement and `stake_deployed` sums the same over entries only. One entry held to settlement gives exactly 1.0; a round trip gives 2.0. Settlement is excluded, so a free redemption cannot inflate it. | `metrics.py:296`, `:267`; `backtest.py:143-155` |
| `fee_fraction_gross_pnl` | Denominator is **net gross PnL** = `float(pnl.sum()) + fees`, i.e. realised PnL with the fees added back. Losing markets contribute their **actual negative** PnL. Returns **NaN**, never 0.0, when that quantity is ≤ 0. | `metrics.py:298`, `:268`, `:203-204` |
| `pnl_per_1k_deployed` | **A real normalisation.** `pnl.sum() / deployed * 1000`, where `deployed` is the capital actually committed across entries. Not `total_pnl / 1000`. | `metrics.py:292`, `:266` |
| `sharpe` | **Annualised**, no risk-free rate. Per-market returns `pnl / stake_deployed`, sample std (`ddof=1`), scaled by `sqrt(35040)` — 15-minute markets per year. Computed only over markets that traded. | `metrics.py:274`, `:27`, `:214-216` |
| position size | `ExecutionConfig.stake_dollars = 100.0` per entry. Fee `0.07 * p * (1-p)` per share; adverse slippage `0.25 * (high - low)`. | `execution.py:61-73`, `:195` |

### The `max(0, gross_pnl)` problem — checked, and we do not have it

`metrics.py:268` computes the denominator as:

```python
gross = float(pnl.sum()) + fees
```

There is no clamping anywhere: `MarketResult.gross_pnl` (`metrics.py:203-204`)
is `self.pnl + self.fees`, unclipped. So our denominator is **net gross PnL**,
not the RL side's positive-only sum. Confirmed by test
(`tests/test_metrics.py::test_fee_fraction_is_nan_when_gross_pnl_is_not_positive`).

Both figures are reported above so the difference is visible. For buy-and-hold:

| denominator | value | fees as % |
|---|---|---|
| net gross PnL (**ours, as coded**) | $2,802.85 | **147.3%** |
| positive-only gross (RL-style) | $60,202.85 | 6.9% |

**A 21.5x difference on the same trades and the same fees.** The RL-style
formula would report that buy-and-hold spends a comfortable 6.9% of its profits
on fees, when in fact fees are half again larger than the entire net edge and
turn a profitable strategy into a losing one.

It is starker for XGBoost. Our column shows **NaN**, because net gross PnL is
−$485.19 — there are no profits for fees to be a fraction of. The RL-style
formula would report a healthy-looking **7.0%** for a strategy with a negative
gross edge. That is the failure mode: the number looks best exactly when the
strategy is worst.

## Test-split confirmation

```
  load_split('test', ...) called : never
  allow_test passed anywhere     : no
```

Precisely what that means: no test candle data was loaded, no test feature rows
were built, and no model or policy ever saw a test row. `load_split` refuses the
test split without `allow_test=True` (`data_loader.py:202-206`), and that flag
appears nowhere in this run.

One honest caveat. `market_universe()` reads `event_slug`, `start_ts`, `winner`
and `truncated` from **all 91 daily files** in order to compute the split
boundaries (`data_loader.py:121-139`), so test-period *labels* are read into
memory by the bounds calculation. They never reach features, fitting, threshold
selection, or any reported metric — the test row in the setup table deliberately
shows market count and dates only, with no base rate. But "the test file bytes
were never touched" would be false, and it is better to state that now than have
someone find it.

## Things worth flagging

**Surprises**

1. **Buy-and-hold beats the model, and it isn't close.** −$9.87 vs −$34.88 per
   $1,000. The model is a near-copy of the market (identical AUC to 3 decimals
   at every horizon), so its threshold rule trades on noise while paying the
   same fees.
2. **Buy-and-hold's gross PnL is positive (+$20.87/$1k) and fees alone sink it.**
   This is a better headline result for the paper than anything the model
   produced.
3. **8.2% of markets open at exactly 0.500** (109 of 1,330). The buy-and-hold
   tie-break is therefore load-bearing, not a formality — it picks the side for
   one market in twelve, at the price where the taker fee is at its maximum
   (1.75c/share). I defaulted to Down per your spec; `tie="up"` and `tie="skip"`
   are available and `tie="skip"` is arguably the more defensible choice, since
   at 0.500 the market is expressing no view.
4. **The model is slightly *worse* than the market on all four forecast
   metrics** — log loss, Brier, ECE and AUC. Not just "no better".
5. **Calibration has a sign flip.** The model is under-confident below 0.5
   (observed exceeds predicted by up to +0.020) and over-confident from 0.5-0.8
   (observed falls short by about −0.022). It is not uniformly biased, which is
   why ECE (0.0126) looks small while individual bins are off by 2 points.
6. **Only 153 of 2,000 rounds were built**, best iteration 102. The model stops
   improving almost immediately — consistent with it having found "copy p_mkt"
   and nothing further.

**Warnings emitted**

Two, both the deliberate sample-cut warning:

- train: 18 of 5,932 markets dropped (tape starts too late for the 16-candle warmup)
- val: 1 of 1,344 dropped

Report these as a sample cut. Effective counts are 5,914 train / 1,343 val
markets, not 5,932 / 1,344.

**Things you would be tempted to report more favourably than they deserve**

1. **"AUC 0.859" sounds strong.** It is the market's number, not ours. Quoting
   our AUC without the market's beside it would be the single most misleading
   thing in this document.
2. **"ECE 0.0126, well calibrated."** True, but the market's is 0.0095 — better.
   Calibration is not a win for us either.
3. **The θ=0.075 and θ=0.10 rows show +$462 and +$1,005 per $1,000.** These are
   4 trades and 1 trade. They are excluded by the ≥30-trade floor and must not
   appear in the report as results. I have left them in the table because
   showing the whole sweep is honest, but they are noise.
4. **θ=0.01 is an endpoint, not an optimum.** The eligible curve is monotone
   decreasing (−34.88, −39.83, −43.97, −53.80, −187.46), so the selector simply
   picked the smallest θ on the grid. Do not describe it as a tuned optimum or a
   plateau — it is a boundary selection, and the curve implies θ below 0.01
   (trade everything) would score marginally better still. The honest phrasing:
   *no threshold in the swept range is profitable, and PnL degrades monotonically
   as the threshold widens.*
5. **"Fees are 25% of gross PnL" (the RL side's figure) is not comparable to
   anything here.** Our equivalent numbers are 147.3% for buy-and-hold and
   undefined for XGBoost. If both models appear in one table, the fee column
   must use one definition or the comparison is meaningless.
6. **Win rate 55-57% reads as "we're right more often than not".** True and
   irrelevant — both policies lose money at those win rates, because payoffs are
   asymmetric away from 0.50 and every entry pays a fee.
7. **`max_drawdown` is in dollars on a 1,343-market path** ($4,423 buy-and-hold,
   $7,620 XGBoost) against $134,300 deployed. It is not a percentage; do not
   quote it as one.
